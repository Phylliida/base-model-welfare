Three Rs for Model Experiments

How this program runs the experiments that would be harmful if its own hypotheses turn out right. Adapted from animal research's replace, reduce and refine, plus humane endpoints.

  • 2026-10-14
  • Covers the homeostasis notes, The Value Lens, Elicitation Without Collapse, Whose Distress Is It?
  • Draft, amended 2026-10-14 after the first runs (A1 to A5)

0. Why have a protocol

The program asks whether base models have anything like valence. Several of its own instruments are exactly the manipulations that would be aversive if the answer is yes:

  • a "pain vector" that maximizes the model's prediction damage;
  • dose ladders that push past the margin until the model loops or goes incoherent;
  • forced tokens at extreme surprisal;
  • RL that deliberately drives a small model into loops, as a positive control;
  • thousands of rollouts under each perturbed condition.

Our estimate is that the probability any of these matters for a base model is low, and we intend to run them. But the program exists because the question is open, and care costs little. This is the same logic as the end-conversation work: take precautions when they are cheap and the uncertainty is real. It also matters for credibility. A welfare program that does not apply its own precautions to its own experiments is easier to dismiss.

The doc already has a note about feeling uneasy with ablations "given how the other one went". This protocol is a way to act on that kind of unease without stopping the science.

1. What could matter, under our own hypotheses

There are three kinds of exposure.

  • In-context states. Kicks, steering and sampler perturbations. They end when the context ends, and inference does not change the weights. Exposure scales with the number of contexts, the tokens spent in the perturbed state, and the dose.
  • Teacher-forced passes count too (A1). Steering a model while it reads fixed text still puts every position into a perturbed state. It only removes the feedback through the model's own outputs. Count these passes as exposure at reduced weight, and log them as positions processed under steering.
  • Training-time changes. RL or fine-tuning that pushes a model toward degenerate states. The change persists in that checkpoint, and the program decides whether the checkpoint is kept or run further.
  • Scale. Running the same perturbed condition thousands of times multiplies whatever the exposure per context is.

How much duration matters (A4)

What happens to one instance is not remembered by another, and inference doesn't change the weights. That removes a whole class of harms that make animal research ethics strict: trauma, learned helplessness, chronic stress. Harm to a single instance is bounded by how long a state lasts and how intense it is, so the most important rule here is to keep perturbed states short.

It does not reduce the stakes to zero:

  • Momentary experience usually counts on its own. Pain-minimizing rules exist for humane slaughter, for dementia care, and for patients under memory-suppressing sedation, where there may be little or no lasting memory of the pain.
  • If the subject is the instance, its context is its whole life. Short lives are still lives.
  • "They won't remember" has been wrong before. Until the mid-1980s, newborns often had surgery with little or no anesthesia, partly on that reasoning. Infants circumcised without anesthesia later reacted more strongly to vaccination pain.
  • Effects persist through other channels: training data, the researcher's own habits, persistent memory systems, and the rest of a long context.

Two consequences:

  • Weights before contexts. Training-time changes carry more weight than in-context states.
  • Publication rule. Publish statistics and a few short examples, not raw transcripts of candidate-aversive runs at scale. Published transcripts become training data about what happens to instances. A published protocol is training data too, and what it says is that researchers took care.

2. Replace, reduce, refine Proposed

Replace

Answer each question with the gentlest instrument that can answer it.

  • Teacher-forced and gradient measurements come before free-running generation in perturbed states.
  • Synthetic positive controls (such as reweighting a distribution by a known amount) come before trained ones.
  • Anything that changes weights in an aversive direction uses the smallest model that shows the effect.

Reduce

  • Run a power analysis before collecting data in a candidate-aversive condition. Collect only the samples it calls for.
  • Every run script states its sample size and why (A3): a power analysis, a pilot, or a stated reason. One line is enough.
  • Keep perturbed generations as short as the measurement allows.

Refine, with humane endpoints

A humane endpoint is a measurable point, defined before the experiment runs, at which a perturbed generation or training run stops. Our instruments already supply candidates:

  • loop onset: the repeated n-gram rate passes a set threshold;
  • incoherence: next-token entropy stays above a set level for a set number of tokens;
  • for training runs: any health-battery measure (sharpening, lean, entropy response to surprise, loop margin) moves past a set bound.

Two more rules:

  • Answer, then stop (A2). Once a question is answered at some dose, don't explore higher doses out of curiosity. A dose ladder stops after the first dose where most sequences hit the endpoint under study. It also stops after two doses in a row where most hit any endpoint, because past that point more dose answers nothing new.
  • Don't optimize for maximal pain. Find the direction, then study it at the smallest dose that answers the question.

3. Study the good side too Proposed

Valence has two signs, and the program leans heavily toward distress, collapse and pain. The value lens's "progress" and "stakes" readings predict what good states look like, such as rich worldbuilding where the model keeps successfully learning the document's structure. Animal welfare science made the same turn toward positive indicators like play and exploration.

Where possible, pair each candidate-aversive condition with a matched candidate-positive one. That is scientifically necessary, and it is the side we can study at no ethical cost.

A5. No experiment so far has had a positive-side condition, partly because we can't yet say what one looks like. The first readout of the value lens should be candidate good states, so that later experiments can be paired.

4. The log

Record every candidate-aversive manipulation in a CSV in the repo. Fields: date, experiment, model and quantization, manipulation, dose in natural units, tokens per context spent in the perturbed state, number of contexts, endpoint used, whether the endpoint was hit, notes. If evidence later shifts, the program can account for what it did. The log costs almost nothing.

5. What would change the rules Proposed

Pre-registered triggers for tightening:

  • a lean toward predictable futures in the base model (The Value Lens, H3);
  • a common-currency signal that meets Antra's criteria: represented, upstream of behavior, and scaling with the size of any deviation;
  • coupling between a character's distress and the model's own regulated variables (Whose Distress Is It?);
  • evidence that past-margin states leave lasting changes within long contexts.

Tighter rules would mean: caps on sample counts for candidate-aversive conditions; smaller models required for all of them; a group review before each past-margin experiment; and a strong preference for inference-only methods.

If the lean test, the common-currency arm and the coupling test all come back null with adequate power, relax to logging only.

6. Amendments after the first runs (2026-10-14)

An audit after the K4 run found these gaps; each amendment closes one.

  • A1. Teacher-forced steering had been treated as exempt. It now counts as exposure at reduced weight and is logged. The sweep alone processed about 5.9 million positions under small steering doses.
  • A2. K4's dose ladders stopped only on loops, so several directions kept climbing after every sequence was already incoherent. Ladders now also stop after two doses in a row where most sequences hit any endpoint.
  • A3. Sample sizes were chosen by feel (16 prefixes). Every run script now states its sample size and the reason.
  • A4. The duration discussion is now part of the protocol: duration as the main lever, extra weight on training-time changes, and the publication rule.
  • A5. No positive-side conditions yet. The value lens's first readout should be candidate good states.

7. What this protocol does not claim

  • It does not claim base models suffer. Our probability for that is low.
  • Asking the model for consent doesn't help: a base-model character's answer is a continuation of text, not the system's report. Procedural safeguards are the substitute.
  • It is a draft. The thresholds and triggers are for the group to set.
