Same Surprise, Different Tail

Plain temperature and a late-layer steering vector can raise the model's average surprise by the same amount. Temperature spills the extra probability into junk tokens. The vector keeps it among plausible ones, until it is pushed too hard.

  • UnsteeredThe model's own next-token distribution.
  • TemperatureEvery probability raised to the power 1/T, with T tuned at each position so the average surprise matches the vector's.
  • VectorThe layer-56 direction the optimizer learned for "favor tokens with p ≈ 0.03", added at every position.
Vector strength

Out of 1,000 sampled tokens, where do they come from?

Each square is one sampled next token. Its shade says how likely the unsteered model thought that token was. Squares are sorted, so very likely tokens fill from the left and junk ends up at the right.

How much is each kind of token boosted?

The same comparison, measured precisely. Each point shows how many times more often tokens of that likelihood get picked than without steering. The dashed line at ×1 means no change.

  • At ×4 the two are equally surprising, but differently shaped. Temperature makes junk tokens (below 1 in 10,000) more than twice as common, and the rarest ones about 300 times more likely. The vector leaves junk where it was and moves the mass into plausible alternatives instead.
  • It works like truncation followed by temperature, the min-p or top-p trick samplers use, which suggests the model internally separates its plausible tail from its junk tail.
  • It is a shape, not a dial for one band. The vector learned for p ≈ 0.1 makes a similar shape that does not center on 0.1.
  • Past about 1× the residual norm it breaks. At ×16 the vector floods the junk tail too, and at ×32 most samples are junk for both.

Check: a steered temperature vector really is temperature

The same measurement can tell the two apart. The layer-50 vector the optimizer learned for a "temperature 1.3" target lands on exact temperature from the top token down to about one in a hundred million. It only falls a bit short in the very deepest tail, which holds almost no probability.
