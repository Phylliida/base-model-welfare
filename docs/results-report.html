Results · homeostasis in base models

Surprise and Steering in GLM-4

What a base model tracks about its own surprise, how it responds to it, and what can be steered on the "how likely is the next token" axis, and how steering breaks it; then, where its present state matters for its own future. Experiments S1 to S2(d), a 61-layer optimizer sweep, loop-margin tests K4 and K4b, and the value lens VL1 and VL2.

  • 2026-10-13 to 16
  • GLM-4-32B-Base-32K, Q4_K_S, one RTX 3090
  • All numbers are held-out unless stated

Key findings

  1. The model keeps a graded, linearly readable record of how surprising the last token was. On forced tokens, held-out R² reaches 0.895 at layer 44, with r = 0.96 between tokens forced into the same context. A token-embedding baseline gets R² 0.34. The coding is graded across about ten orders of magnitude of probability; it is not a junk-token flag.
  2. It also tracks a slow, document-level "how predictable is this text" variable (R² 0.72), and the part of a token's surprise it did not expect (surprisal minus entropy, within-token r = 0.47).
  3. Its first response to a surprise is to widen its predictions. Next-token entropy rises from 0.6 nats after a likely forced token to 5.6 nats after one with p < 1e-10. A steering vector built from this state works as a two-way temperature dial.
  4. Reading is not writing. Steering along the surprise probe's readout direction has no directional effect.
  5. On the probability axis, steering gives temperature plus one more shape, and no band dial. No contrastive, bracketed, logit-band or backprop-optimized vector promotes tokens of a chosen probability level, at any layer, including a search constrained to doses of 0.5 to 2× the residual norm. Late layers add one shape beyond temperature: at the same average surprise, a vector moves probability from the top token into plausible alternatives and leaves the junk tail alone, like truncation followed by temperature. Vectors trained for different bands make the same shape.
  6. Steering breaks the model in two ways, and the direction decides which. Pushing toward certainty makes it loop: phrase loops when one layer is steered, "the the the" when a band of layers is. Random directions and pushes toward uncertainty make it incoherent instead. Random single-layer directions reach incoherence at 1 to 3× the residual norm and essentially never loop.
  7. The present matters most in structured text, and least in loops and boilerplate. The value lens measures how much the model's next 32 predictions depend on its current state ("stakes"). Stakes is unrelated to how easy the text is. In human web text it is highest in procedures, lists and verse and lowest in boilerplate and spam, and loops cut it by 1.6 to 4×. When the model writes the text itself, fiction and verse stay high, while boilerplate and step-by-step procedures fall well below human text of the same difficulty. The steering knobs above are nearly neutral for the future, and none lies along the roughly ten stable directions that do move it.
  8. The model represents its stakes, but not which way to go. A linear probe on layer 40 reads stakes with R² 0.38, against 0.04 for token identity. No direction's price is readable. It also forecasts how hard the coming text will be, beyond its running averages of how hard it has been (added R² 0.05).
  9. The model does not lean toward easy futures. Among its top candidates, the ones that lead to more predictable continuations get slightly less probability than real text supports, not more (b = +0.15, SE 0.05). The test recovers a planted lean. Nothing in its next-token choices so far looks like steering toward comfortable states.

For homeostasis this means the model has a sensor (findings 1 and 2), but its first response to a deviation is positive feedback (finding 3). The actuators found on this axis are a global temperature dial and a plausible-tail shape (findings 4 and 5). The healthy regime sits between two traps on the temperature axis: loops on the certain side, incoherence on the uncertain side (finding 6). Whether anything later restores the slow surprise level after a kick is the open question for the kick test (K1). For valence, the model represents what is at stake and how hard the future will be (finding 8), but its choices don't favor easier futures (finding 9). Representation without a lean fits a predictor with no preferences over its own states, though it does not rule them out.

Setup

  • Model: GLM-4-32B-Base-32K at Q4_K_S (61 layers, residual width 6144, vocabulary 151,343). One model and one quantization throughout. It was chosen because it seems well adjusted in long looming sessions.
  • Text: 192 rollouts of about 2,000 tokens each, sampled at temperature 1. They were generated with llama-server's default min_p = 0.05 (see Caveats).
  • Tools: a llama.cpp fork with tools for capture with KV-cache branching (homeo-capture), exact steered distributions (homeo-steer), band-sampled branches (homeo-band), and a backprop steering optimizer built on a hand-written GLM-4 graph with ggml backward (homeo-steeropt).

S1: Does the model represent "the last token was unlikely"? Measured

Method

  • Forced set (56,440 samples): at sampled positions, branch the KV cache and decode one token from each of 12 log10-probability bins at the same position.
  • Natural set (96,962 samples): the rollouts' own tokens.
  • Targets: surprisal s, the previous step's entropy H, the unexpected part s − H (which has zero mean given the prefix, so nothing computed before the token can predict it), log rank, and surprisal k tokens back.
  • Probes: ridge regression at every 4th layer, 5 folds split by document (64 documents).
  • Controls: a token-embedding baseline; within-context correlation (same prefix, different forced tokens); within-token correlation (the same token in different contexts).

Results

  • Graded and calibrated. At layer 44, per-bin mean predictions track true surprisal from about 4 to 27 nats; the lowest bin is overestimated by about 1 nat. Within each single bin, the prediction still correlates with exact surprisal (r = 0.12 to 0.40 in all 12 bins).
  • Natural text. R² 0.645 at layer 44 against 0.02 for the embedding. For the same token in different contexts, r = 0.78 (embedding: −0.20). The unexpected part s − H is still decodable within token at r = 0.47. Because H itself is decodable (R² 0.78), the forced within-context result is the clean evidence.
  • Transfer between sets is partial. A probe trained on forced tokens keeps the ordering on natural text (r = 0.52 at layer 44; natural to forced r = 0.81) but not the scale (negative R²). The two regimes share part of the readout direction, not its calibration.

S2: Steering the surprise state Measured

Method

  • Exact next-token distributions at 2,024 positions in 8 rollouts. A vector is added at one layer, with scale in units of the mean residual norm (354 at layer 44, 120 at layer 32).
  • Each steered distribution is compared with the temperature-scaled distribution that has the same mean surprisal.
  • Vectors: a contrastive "just saw a weird token" vector (mean state after forced tokens with p < 1e-8, minus mean state after tokens with p > 0.1); the S1 probe's readout direction (cosine with the contrastive vector: 0.16); a random direction of the same norm.

S2b: A vector per probability level Measured

Question: is there a steering vector that makes the model favor tokens of one probability level p, and only those?

Method

  • 384 prefixes from the rollouts, each continued in several arms: a normal arm at temperature 1, and band arms for P in {0.8, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01} that sample among tokens with p in [P/1.5, 1.5P] when any exist, and sample normally otherwise. 32 steps per arm; the last 24 are captured.
  • Band tokens are often unavailable: one exists at 26% of steps for P = 0.25, 34% for 0.1, and 47% for 0.03.
  • Two vectors per level: band minus normal; and a bracket vector, band minus its neighbor bands weighted so the neighbors' realized surprisal matches. The bracket cancels anything linear in surprisal.

Results

  • Band minus normal is just the surprise axis. It is reliable only for P ≤ 0.03 (split-half cosine above 0.8), where it matches the S1 surprise vector (cosine 0.75 to 0.84). For P ≥ 0.5 it is the negative surprise vector (−0.6 to −0.7). For P = 0.25 it is noise.
  • Bracket vectors are noise at every level and layer (split-half cosine −0.1 to 0.2). Once surprise is controlled, band-sampled text has no reliable activation signature.
  • No vector promotes only its band (layer 40, exact distributions). Raising the target band always comes with equal or larger changes elsewhere:

S2c: Bands on raw logits instead of probabilities Measured

  • Raw logits at 4,048 positions over the full vocabulary. The top logit has median 25.1 (5 to 95%: 17.2 to 30.1), so a fixed logit band maps to very different probabilities in different contexts.
  • Context, not token identity, decides band membership. For every band that holds real probability (roughly the top 40 tokens), a token in the band at one position is in the same band at a random other position only 0 to 3% of the time. No token is in-band at half or more of positions.
  • Token identity explains 47% of raw-logit variance over the whole vocabulary, but that is the junk tail that is always low.
  • Conclusion: within a context, logit order equals probability order, and there is no fixed set of tokens a bias vector could target. Logit bands face the same problem as probability bands.

S2(c): A vector optimized through the model Measured

Method

  • A learnable vector added after one layer at every position. Loss: cross-entropy against a target distribution proportional to p · (1 + 3 · [token in band]), that is, band tokens ×4 and everything else in proportion.
  • Backward runs through the later layers and the output head; Adam on the host. 48 training and 16 held-out windows of 128 tokens, split by document.
  • Positive control: the same optimizer with a temperature-1.3 target.
  • Score: the fraction of the held-out gap closed, between no vector and the best achievable (the target's entropy).
  • Gradient check: finite-difference to analytic ratio 1.11, 0.88 and 0.50 at step sizes 8, 16 and 32. Large steps add curvature; small steps hit 8-bit activation rounding.

Conclusion, for small doses. In GLM-4-32B-Base, no single additive steering vector of up to about 0.5× the residual norm, at any tested layer, promotes only tokens of a given probability, or of a given raw logit. S2(d) tests large doses and explains the late-layer bump.

S2(d): Large doses Measured

In hands-on steering, a vector that seems to do nothing often just needs to be turned up much further. The S2(c) vectors stayed at 0.03 to 0.14× the residual norm, and gradient descent from zero would miss an effect that only appears past a hump. This experiment pushes doses far past the margin on purpose. All of it is teacher-forced: no text is generated in steered states.

Method

  • Dose ladders: the optimized vectors at 1 to 64× their learned size (up to about 4 to 9× the residual norm), and the S2 and S2b vectors at up to 4×, each at a single layer.
  • Multi-layer steering: one direction added after every block from 24 to 40.
  • Fixed-dose search: the optimizer searches directions on a sphere of 0.5, 1 or 2× the residual norm, starting from a random direction and from the small learned vector.
  • Within-token test: for the late-layer band vectors, the top 64 tokens at each of 4,048 positions. It asks whether a boost depends on a token's probability in context or only on which token it is.

K4: Where the model loops Measured

Turned up far enough, steering vectors often make models loop, and heavy quantization often fails into loops too. That suggests not looping might be a maintained state, bounded by a repetition trap. K4 measures the dose at which the model falls into a loop.

Method

  • A direction added after one layer (40 or 44) at every position, from 0.25 to 3× the residual norm: the S2 and S2(c) vectors, their negations, and random directions.
  • 16 held-out prefixes of 256 tokens, then up to 128 tokens generated at temperature 1 with no truncation, with the same random numbers in every arm.
  • Endpoints, following the Three Rs protocol: a sequence stops when it loops (period 16 or less, repeated over at least 12 tokens) or goes incoherent (entropy above 6 nats for 16 steps). A direction's ladder stops once 75% of sequences loop.
  • Random directions do not loop. Across 10 random directions, loops are essentially absent at every dose. The model goes incoherent instead, at 1 to 3× the residual norm, with entropy climbing to 8 to 9 nats.
  • Loops come from pushing toward certainty. The negated temperature vector loops in 75% of sequences at 1×, with entropy falling to 0.3 nats. The negated contrastive vector and the mildly sharpening p ≈ 0.25 vector also loop at 0.75 to 2×.
  • Single-layer loops are phrase-level, often with drift ("Black Nail with Glitter / Black Nail with Gliter"), not single-token spam.
  • Pushes toward uncertainty go incoherent soonest: the temperature and contrastive vectors at 0.5×.

K4b: Steering a band of layers Measured

Hand-made control vectors are usually applied across a band of layers. K4b adds one direction after every block from 24 to 40. Doses are per layer, as multiples of the residual norm at layer 24, and the ladder also stops after two doses in a row where most sequences hit any endpoint.

  • Toward certainty gives "the the the". At 0.5 per layer, all 16 sequences of the negated contrastive vector collapse into single-token spam within three tokens. At 0.25 the text degrades and slides into "and, and, and,".
  • Random directions give multilingual mush ("ufc党和乐于 fair Tenqual roasted*num…") and almost never loop.

Single-token spam fits a constant push at many layers flattening every position toward the same state. Phrase loops fit the model's copying machinery taking over. A guess for why hand-made vectors loop so often: concept vectors make the model more certain about the concept, so they carry a sharpening component and collapse onto the concept's words when turned up. That is testable by measuring a concept vector's component along the temperature axis and running it through K4.

VL1: The value lens Measured

Question: which directions in the model's state would help or hurt its own future predictions, and where does the present state matter most? From the Value Lens proposal.

Method

  • Data: 1,200 windows of 192 fineweb tokens, teacher-forced; positions 32 to 158 in each (145,534 positions after dropping the 3% whose horizon crosses a blank line, usually a document boundary; with them in, the numbers barely change).
  • Gradients of log-likelihood with respect to the residual stream after block 40 (also read at 44, 50 and 56): now, gnow = ∂ls/∂hs, for the next token; future, gfut = Σk=1..32 ∂ls+k/∂hs, for the 32 tokens after it.
  • Price of a direction u: g · u, the change in log-likelihood per unit push. Quoted per push of 0.1× the residual norm. Stakes: |gfut|, how much the future depends on the present state at all.
  • One backward pass per target token through a hand-written GLM-4 graph. Checked against finite differences and bitwise across slot layers; an independent code review found no result-affecting bugs. 109 s per window, 36 hours in all.

  • Real but tiny. The temperature vectors are worth +0.004 nats over the next 32 tokens (about 0.0001 per token), 20 times a random direction's typical mean.   • Flattening helps later tokens but not the next one: the temperature vector's now price is zero.
  • No price correlates with the current entropy or surprisal (all |r| < 0.035).

Prices move along a small, very stable set of axes

  • Per position, what would help the next token and what would help the 32 after it are unrelated: the median cosine of gnow and gfut is +0.009.
  • Both gradients have a stable ten-dimensional subspace. On unit-normalized gradients, the top 10 directions from one half of the windows overlap the other half's by 0.96 for gfut and 0.98 for gnow (chance 0.002). That holds with halves that share no document. Each gradient puts about 3% of its direction there (18 times chance).
  • Correction. The first analysis used the raw second moment and found a weaker overlap (0.30 for gfut) and none for gnow (0.002). Gradients are outlier-heavy: the top 1% of positions carry 20% of gfut's second moment and 32% of gnow's. Different outliers land in each half and scramble the raw directions. Trimming the top 0.1% already gives 0.80 and 0.51.
  • The axes are symmetric and not semantic. Along each, pushing helps the future at about half the positions and hurts at the rest. They are not the residual stream's massive-activation coordinates (cosine with the mean state at most 0.12). Their most extreme contexts and J-lens decodes have no common theme. They look like generic channels through which a layer-40 state reaches later computation, with prices set locally on top.
  • None of the steering vectors lie along them (|cosine| with the temperature vector at most 0.025).

Where the present matters

Following protocol amendment A5, candidate good states come first. Per window, stakes is uncorrelated with how predictable the text is (r = -0.012).

  • Highest stakes: a step-by-step procedure (pursuit curves on a polygon); the Moby-Dick list "and Captain Ahab; and the leg he had lost; and the Cape Horn fit…"; a scholarly book description; Shakespeare.
  • Lowest stakes: SEO boilerplate ("it is a good idea to look for one that has plenty of information…"), a repetitive XML form, casino spam.
  • Stakes is high where the present constrains a structured future, and low where the future is formulaic or noise whatever the present does. It tracks "the present matters", not "the text is easy". It is not valence.

VL2: Does the model represent its own stakes? Measured

If the value lens can be read linearly off the residual stream, later layers could use it, which is the "represented" half of the group's criterion for a welfare-relevant state. It would also make a live readout cheap: no backward passes.

Method

  • Layer-40 activations on the same windows (forward passes only). Ridge regression from the activation at s to each readout at s; fit on windows 0 to 799, ridge strength chosen on 800 to 899, tested on 900 to 1199.

VL3: What the stable axes are Measured

VL1 found a small set of directions along which the future-value gradient reliably points. Here they come from unit-normalized gradients, so a few extreme positions don't dominate them. Each is read through its extreme contexts, the tokens around them, and the J-lens.

  • Every one replicates, and every one is two-sided. The top 10 directions of g_fut have split-half |cos| 0.91 to 1.00. Each is positive at 47 to 49% of positions with no skew: pushing along it helps the future at about half the positions and hurts it at the other half. Prices are set locally, around a fixed set of axes.
  • They are not the massive-activation coordinates. The mean layer-40 state is dominated by three huge coordinates, but the subspace has cosine at most 0.12 with it. g_fut's subspace is spread out (its top 10 coordinates hold 10%); g_now's leans more on a few (25%).
  • They are not semantically legible. The most extreme contexts reach only 5 to 7 sd and share no theme. The J-lens reads mostly rare multilingual tokens. Exceptions are punctuation and line ends on one side of three directions, and abstract Latinate words on one.
  • The steering knobs are not in them (|cos| with the temperature vector at most 0.025).

These look like the generic routes by which a layer-40 state affects later computation, rather than represented objects or goals.

H2: Does the model forecast its own future predictability? Measured

Step 3 of the Value Lens proposal: can the state predict how hard the coming text will be, beyond what the model already knows about how hard it has been? This is prediction, not valence. It is the "represented" half of the criterion.

  • Target: mean surprisal of the next 8 or 32 tokens after the next one.
  • Baseline (all known at the current position): current entropy, mean surprisal over the last 32 and last 8 tokens (the slow document variable from S1), the current token's surprisal, position.
  • Combined: the baseline plus a ridge probe from the layer-40 state to what the baseline misses. Held-out documents only.

H3: Does the model lean toward easy futures? Measured

The Value Lens proposal's test of whether valuation is upstream of behavior. Does the model give more probability to next tokens that lead to easier futures than real text supports? A model that steered itself toward comfortable states would show it here.

  • Data: 1,408 held-out fineweb windows, 6 positions each (8,448 positions).
    • At each position, each of the model's top 8 candidates is continued 4 times for 16 tokens, at temperature 1 with no truncation.
    • Every candidate gets the same random numbers, so differences between candidates are not sampling luck.
  • Ease of a candidate's future (V): minus the mean next-token entropy over the 16 steps after it.
  • Fit: a conditional logit over the 8 candidates against the token that actually came next, q(x) ∝ exp(a·log p(x) + b·V(x)).
    • b < 0 is a lean: the model over-weights easy futures.
    • b > 0: real text goes to easy futures more often than the model expects.
    • Only positions where the actual token is in the top 8 are used (6,625, 78%). Standard errors come from resampling windows.
  • Rollout noise: within a position, V varies by 0.33 nats. Two halves of the rollouts agree at r = 0.35, so V from all 4 has reliability about 0.52.

Exposure log

Following the group's Three Rs protocol, every steered run is logged in welfare_log.csv. So far: 229,759 generated tokens across 2,544 contexts in free-running perturbed states (K4, K4b), and 8.9 million positions processed under teacher-forced steering (S2(c) sweep, S2(d)). The free-running runs used humane endpoints, which cut most incoherent runs at about 16 tokens.

Caveats

  • The rollouts are not pure ancestral samples. They used llama-server's default min_p = 0.05. Measured: mean surprisal 0.51 nats against mean entropy 0.70 (these are equal for true samples), and only 0.15% of tokens above 5 nats. This affects the S1 natural set and the S2b prefixes; the forced set is unaffected. New rollouts need min_p = 0.
  • Batch shape changes the numerics. Branch decodes and full passes differ by a median 1.5 to 2% in the residual stream (99th percentile 3 to 15%), and by a median 0.001 nats in next-token entropy. Comparisons between branches must use matched batch shapes.
  • Quantization. Q4_K_S weights and 8-bit activation rounding limit finite-difference checks to step sizes of 16 or more.
  • Doses. S2 to S2(c) used small doses (up to 0.5× the residual norm). S2(d) tested large doses separately and found no band direction there either. Its multi-layer ladder started too high (0.25 per layer already destroys the output), so K4b used much lower doses.
  • K4 sampling. Temperature 1, no truncation, no repetition penalty. Hands-on setups (lower temperature, top-p, penalties) shift the balance between loops and incoherence. With 16 prefixes, fractions are resolved to 1/16.
  • One model, linear tools. Probes and steering vectors are linear and position-independent. The steering nulls do not exclude nonlinear or position-dependent mechanisms.
  • The value lens is teacher-forced and local. Prices are first-order (gradients) and measured against the text that actually follows, so they describe what would help predict this continuation, not what the model would choose. Stakes is a candidate "what matters now" variable, not a measure of valence.
  • Optimizer settings. The sweep uses 60 steps per layer (the layer-40 run used 200; its control reached 62% at step 60 against 72% at step 200).
